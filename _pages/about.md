I am a historian of medieval/early modern Iberian and global Catholicism, specializing in Christian-Jewish relations. My research interests include the theory and practice of religious conversion, the development and use of Catholic liturgy in missionary contexts, and the modern afterlives of historical Christian anti-Jewish texts. I am a seasoned instructor, having taught four upper-level courses at my current institution (the University of Florida) and a wide variety of public-facing and secondary school courses. I am slated to defend my doctoral dissertation in March 2027. I am currently seeking a faculty position starting in the fall of 2027.

My fully-drafted dissertation, entitled “Paul of Burgos and the Jewish Christian Community of Castile c. 1400,” nuances traditional assumptions about a strict medieval boundary between Judaism and Christianity. I argue that the rabbi-turned-bishop Paul of Burgos viewed himself as meaningfully Jewish and Christian, and that Paul of Burgos developed, pioneered, and implemented a theology and practice of Jewish Christianity that enjoyed wide currency both in his own generation and among later conversos. Methodologically, this project aims to unite literary and archival sources about Paul of Burgos that have never been read together; in addition to a close reading of his corpus of Latin and Hebrew writings, I am also heavily drawing upon extensive archival research in Burgos, Madrid, Rome, and other Castilian archives. This dissertation research received funding from the [Medieval Academy of America](https://www.themedievalacademyblog.org/maa-news-fellows-research-awards-5/), the University of Florida History Department, and the University of Florida Hamilton School. I have published aspects of this research in [Church History](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/church-history/article/renarrating-judaism-a-new-reading-of-the-additiones-of-pablo-de-santa-maria/DB5148B2CF458970F3DBEFA0E95FC000) and in Medieval Encounters (forthcoming).

My ongoing research agenda explores the broader Spanish and global impact of late medieval/early modern attempts to contest the boundaries of Christianity. In an article in the [Journal of Early Modern History](https://brill.com/view/journals/jemh/aop/article-10.1163-15700658-bja10117/article-10.1163-15700658-bja10117.xml), I presented and analyzed the neglected liturgical texts of 15th-16th century Arabic Masses, arguing that these vernacular liturgies - instituted as an embodiment of converso theories of liturgical inculturation - shaped the ongoing development of Morisco Christianity. As I have argued in [academic](https://ejournals.bc.edu/index.php/scjr/issue/view/1451) and [public-facing](https://www.pillarcatholic.com/p/evangelization-in-the-15th-century) articles, the 15th century was a key moment in theorizing the shape of Christianity that continues to resonate today.

At the University of Florida, I have taught upper-level classes on the History of Christianity in [late antiquity](https://archive.syllabus.ufl.edu/WOH-3239-History-of-Christianity-2-Z.-Young-.pdf) through the [early](https://archive.syllabus.ufl.edu/WOH-3239-History-of-Christianity-2-Z.-Young-.pdf) [modern](https://archive.syllabus.ufl.edu/WOH3239-History-of-Christianity-Z.-Young-Summer-B-.pdf) period as well as a class on [medieval and modern concepts of holy war](https://zyounghistorian.github.io/zyounghistorian/files/EUH4123 syllabus.pdf) (currently). My teaching philosophy focuses on [empowering students to connect historical texts with contemporary practices](https://zyounghistorian.github.io/zyounghistorian/files/EUH4123syllabus.pdf) and on [facilitating student engagement with primary sources](https://zyounghistorian.github.io/zyounghistorian/files/EUH4123 syllabus.pdf); I have consistently earned [strong](https://zyounghistorian.github.io/zyounghistorian/files/EUH4123syllabus.pdf) [teaching](https://zyounghistorian.github.io/zyounghistorian/files/Spring2026Evals.pdf) [reviews](https://zyounghistorian.github.io/zyounghistorian/files/Summer2025Evals.pdf). I have also taught classes on the history of Christianity, European history, and American history to broader audiences in both secular, ecumenical, and confessional contexts.
